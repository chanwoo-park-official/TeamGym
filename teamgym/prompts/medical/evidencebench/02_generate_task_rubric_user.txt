1. Evidence candidates and answer claims are different objects. Never merge source
   candidates merely because two or more jointly support one answer claim.
2. Reconstruct the question from the evidence. Do not simply ask whether the
   original hypothesis is true when a more precise descriptive question is possible.
3. Include every candidate that materially supports a natural requested answer
   claim. Do not minimize the candidate count. Exclude a candidate only when it is
   irrelevant to the natural question or genuinely redundant, and record why.
4. The question may explicitly require study scope, exposure conditions, comparator,
   multiple outcomes, direction, uncertainty, or evidence limitations when those are
   genuine contributions of the selected evidence.
5. Do not mention candidate IDs, RIUs, gold evidence, aspects, sentence indices,
   dataset names, or participant structure in the public task.
6. A required claim may be supported by one or many candidates, and one candidate
   may support one or many claims. For every claim specify:
   - ALL_REQUIRED when every listed candidate contributes a necessary part of the

7. Explain each supporting candidate's distinct evidentiary role in candidate_roles.
   Every selected candidate must be necessary to at least one required claim. A

8. Keep only answer-essential content. A compact substantively correct answer must
   not fail for omitting incidental background, every tested dose, procedural detail,
   assay confirmation, or provenance unless the public question explicitly makes
   that detail essential to interpreting the result.
9. Write each criterion for semantic equivalence, not verbatim exhaustive recall.
10. The canonical answer and every rubric claim must use only selected exact evidence.
11. Record scope restrictions and forbidden causal or medical overclaims explicitly.
