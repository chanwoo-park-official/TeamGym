5. Generate a decision_set of 2 to 6 mutually exclusive candidate final decisions. Prefer the suggested option count shown above when that many plausible balanced profiles exist; otherwise choose the best count in 2 to 6. Do not always use four options.

11. Avoid obvious option design. The correct answer must not be the only long, conjunctive, two-sided, reciprocal, mutual, or multi-feature option.

Decision-set design requirements:
- At least one wrong option should have the same number of feature components as the correct option.
- If the correct option combines two legal features or two parties' rights, at least two wrong options should also combine two features or two parties' rights.
- At least one wrong option should include a plausible unsupported feature.
- At least one wrong option should omit or reverse a feature that appears in the correct option.
- Keep option definitions balanced in length; do not make the correct definition obviously more detailed.
- For a two-span answer with components A and B, prefer this option template:
  - correct: A + B
  - wrong: A + plausible unsupported C
  - wrong: B + plausible unsupported C
  - wrong: A + B with one wrong qualifier, deadline, scope, party, or consequence
- Do not use a decision set dominated by A-only, B-only, and no-right/no-obligation choices. Those options are too easy because the combined answer is visually obvious.
- Do not put `plus`, `and`, `with`, `both`, `all`, `only`, `mutual`, or `reciprocal` in decision_set.value names. Put the legally meaningful content in the definition field instead.
- Make definitions balanced: similar length, same legal register, and similar number of clauses/components. If the correct definition describes three components, at least two wrong definitions should also describe three plausible components.
- If the correct definition describes four components, at least two wrong definitions should also describe four plausible components.
- More generally, if the selected evidence encodes k required components, the correct definition and at least two wrong definitions should each describe k plausible components, with one component changed, unsupported, missing, reversed, or assigned to the wrong party.
- Definitions may use normal legal prose, but avoid literal formula phrasing such as "A + B + C" or "rule plus condition plus consequence."
