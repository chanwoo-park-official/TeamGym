Selection rules:
1. Select complete candidate blocks by candidate_block_id. Never select an
   individual value or cell from inside a block.
2. One normalized candidate block becomes one candidate PIU. A merged block
   already represents consecutive parser fragments of one source unit.
3. Identify a coherent set of related source QAs and candidate blocks whose
   facts share the same company, period, transaction, segment, liquidity topic,
   accounting policy, operating trend, or filing-review objective.
4. Select blocks within the stated range that combine through related calculations,
   comparisons, reconciliations, classifications, or filing-specific
   conclusions.
   Mathematical combinability alone is insufficient: the relationship and
   resulting conclusion must have a defensible meaning under the source labels,
   scopes, periods, units, and disclosure.
5. Do not select two blocks solely for information they duplicate.
6. Partially overlapping blocks may both be selected only when the task
   explicitly uses a unique contribution from each.
7. Every selected PIU must be answer-determining. If it is unavailable, at
   least one required response item or grounded claim must not be recoverable,
   so a complete response is impossible.
8. A selected block may support multiple rubric claims, and one claim may use
   multiple selected blocks.
9. Do not copy the source QA statement as the new task.

Task rules:
1. Ask one coherent question. When the question has multiple required response
   items, state each item explicitly and relate them to the same company,
   transaction, period, accounting topic, or filing-review objective.
2. Do not expose PIUs, candidate IDs, context IDs, annotations, participants,
   source labels, or construction metadata.
3. Do not give away a requested item, including counts stated in words. For
   example, do not say "the four quarterly periods" while asking how many
   reported periods there are. Reporting years/dates may define scope; they
   must not supply a requested answer value or conclusion.
   State any operation convention needed for an unambiguous answer, but do not
   disclose source values in the public question.
4. Preserve exact entity, segment, period, unit, sign, scope, and precision.
   Do not reinterpret, double-count, or combine source facts in a way that their
   labels, scopes, periods, units, or disclosure do not justify.
5. Every reasoning step must have a stable claim_id. Its uses must be both
   complete and minimal: include every PIU needed for that claim and no PIU that
   supports only another claim or background framing.
6. Across all reasoning steps, every selected PIU must be used. Every requested
   response item must map to one or more reasoning claim IDs.
7. Use a composite answer_spec for multiple required response items. Each item
   must have an item_id, claim_ids, and its own numeric, text, or classification
   grading specification.
8. For numeric items, provide value, unit, and a narrow defensible absolute
   tolerance. For text/classification items, provide accepted_conclusions.
9. The canonical response must answer every required item and show the complete
   source-grounded derivation.
10. The public task must not be answerable from general financial knowledge or
   wording alone.
11. Use feedback to revise the previous selection and task. Do not merely repeat
    a known failed design.
